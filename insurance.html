Insurance Risk & Claims Analysis

A portfolio and claims analysis where the most interesting finding was a data problem, not a chart.

Dataset37,542 policies
Total claims$187.8M
ToolsPower BI, Power Query, DAX
StatusCompleted

View on GitHub ↗

[01]Overview

An auto insurance risk and claims dashboard covering portfolio risk, driver demographics, vehicle characteristics and loss performance. I also audited the data and documented what did not add up.

[02]Business questions
  • Does driver age change claim frequency?
  • Where is loss exposure concentrated by vehicle use and brand?
  • Which demographic segments dominate the portfolio?
  • Can the claim metrics be trusted as defined?
[03]Dataset and validation

37,542 rows with 37,541 unique IDs. One duplicate record was identified and investigated. Claim amount, claim frequency, policy, age and vehicle fields were checked during exploratory analysis.

[05]Data quality investigation
Finding: 27,203 records (72.45%) have a claim amount above zero but a claim frequency of zero. Only 10,339 policies (about 27.5%) have both above zero.

The domain definition says claim amount is the total payout and claim frequency is the count of claims filed, so the two should agree. I treated this as a potential business or data logic issue that deserves investigation, not a confirmed error. The records were retained for risk analysis and the issue was documented and flagged for review, with a warning against reading the claim rate in isolation.

A second observation: some car models map to more than one car make, so model-level metrics should be read together with the make.

[06]Key findings
  • Age: claim rates are nearly flat across age groups, from 27.1% (60+) to 28.0% (under 25). Age alone is not a strong differentiator here.
  • Segments: 40 to 60 is the largest group (about 13.4K records, around $67M in claims), then 25 to 40 (about 11.3K, $56M), 60+ (about 10.1K, $51M) and under 25 (about 2.8K, $14M).
  • Vehicle use: private vehicles account for $150.4M (80.1%) of claims against $37.4M for commercial.
  • Brands: Ford ($16.6M) and Chevrolet ($14.8M) lead total loss, then Dodge ($9.3M) and Toyota ($9.0M).
  • Profile: single drivers are the biggest segment (19.4K policies); Bachelors degree holders account for the largest payout ($94.0M).
[07]Interpretation and recommendations
  • Penalizing younger drivers on age alone may not match actual claim behavior in this data.
  • Confirm the claim frequency definition before using claim rate for pricing decisions.
[08]Limitations

The claim frequency inconsistency is unresolved, so metrics built on it carry that caveat. Findings describe this dataset only.
