Global Superstore Sales & Profit Analysis

Exploratory analysis in Python of what drives sales and profit in a global retail dataset.

Dataset51,290 orders
Period2011 to 2014
ToolsPython, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib
StatusCompleted

View on GitHub ↗

[01]Overview

A Jupyter notebook that goes from raw data to business insights: loading, exploring, cleaning, exploratory analysis, visualization and a written summary. Analysis areas: overall sales, profit and margin, category, region, customer, product, time and discounts.

[02]Business questions
  • Which regions and categories drive sales, and which drive profit?
  • Does higher sales volume mean higher profitability?
  • How do discounts relate to profit and to sales?
  • Which products sell well but lose money?
[03]Data preparation

Data type conversion, duplicate checks and missing-value assessment before analysis. Large negative-profit observations were treated as business cases to investigate, not removed as errors.

[05]Key findings
  • Growth: sales rose every year, from $2.26M in 2011 to $4.30M in 2014. Profit rose from $249K to $504K.
  • Region: Central had the highest sales ($2.82M) but its margin was about 11.0%, below North Asia (about 19.5%). Southeast Asia sold $884K with only $17.9K profit (about 2.0%). Margins here are computed from the notebook's regional table.
  • Category: Technology earned the most profit, ahead of Office Supplies and Furniture.
  • Discounts: average profit fell as discounts rose, and turned clearly negative above roughly 30%. Discounts showed no clear positive link to average sales.
  • Products: some products sell a lot but earn little or lose money (for example a Cubify 3D printer at about -$8.9K), which is why sales should not be judged without profit.
[06]Interpretation

Revenue and margin tell different stories. A region or product can lead on sales and still be a weak profit contributor, and heavy discounting is the most visible source of lost profit in this data. A pricing and discount review would be a sensible first step.

[07]Limitations

Findings come from descriptive and exploratory analysis. They show association, not causation, and the dataset is a sample retail dataset rather than a live business.
